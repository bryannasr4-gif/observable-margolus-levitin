Phage Discovery Engine

A machine that turns the public phage corpus into ranked, falsifiable bench experiments — and a design for keeping it honest enough to deserve the bench time.

Prepared for Pride Laboratories, San Diego Scope Taxon-agnostic, public-corpus-first Objective Novel mechanism, publishable

The argument

Six moves from the seed idea to something worth building

01

The public corpus doubled to ~29,000 complete phage genomes while its novel species-level fraction declined. In a corpus like that, "conspicuously absent" is mostly "not yet sequenced" — so the seed's headline mechanism has to be demoted.

02

A material share of phage "dark matter" is annotation failure, not unknown biology: stop-codon recoding and a 100-codon gene-calling floor. Phage λ — the most studied phage on Earth — hides 55 unannotated translated ORFs.

03

Two Science 2026 papers just did "protein language models discover bacterial defense systems." That lane is closed — but one validated 42 of 94 candidates at the bench, handing us a 44.7% calibration anchor.

04

The phage side is roughly 100× less explored than the bacterial side, for a methodological reason: 80% of anti-defense proteins fall under 200 amino acids, where homology search, PLM embeddings and structure prediction all degrade at once.

05

So build a phage-side, small-protein-biased, target-assigning engine. Not "is this anti-defense?" but "does this inhibit Thoeris?" — which turns a panel of strains into a single plate.

06

And keep it honest by running the entire pipeline on a null corpus to count how many compelling hypotheses it manufactures from noise, treating phylogenetic non-independence as a first-class quantity, and spending a fifth of bench capacity on experiments it expects to fail.

Phase 0

What the seed got wrong

Four mechanisms were proposed. One survives intact, one is largely already published, one is partly mis-specified, and one has to be demoted outright.

Seed mechanismVerdictReasoning
AbsenceDemotedA doubling corpus with a falling novel fraction cannot separate absent from unsampled. Survives only behind an explicit sampling-power model.
Dark matter structureMis-specifiedClustering is done (PHROGs, BFVD, phold). Part of the residue is recoding and small-ORF blindness — annotation failure, cheaper to fix than biology is to discover.
Cross-taxon analogyUpheldThis is the one job LLMs demonstrably do well. The cf-PICI tail-piracy result was exactly this, and it worked.
CouplingUpheld, phage sideDefense–defense co-occurrence across 42,925 genomes is published. Phage-gene × host-defense coupling, properly corrected, is open.

One structural observation. Absence, dark matter and coupling are not three mechanisms — they are three readings of a single sparse matrix of gene families × genomes: read for holes, for unlabelled rows, for correlated columns. Only cross-taxon analogy is genuinely different, because it operates on literature rather than on the matrix. That collapse is useful: one well-corrected substrate serves three mechanisms, and the language-model layer sits orthogonally on top instead of being wired into each.

Mechanisms

What the machine actually looks for

Graded honestly. Tier A is what I would build; each carries its falsifier and, more importantly, its prior-art risk — because a mechanism whose prior art I haven't checked is a liability, not an asset.

A1

Hotspot occupancy → target assignment

Build
Finds
Phage genomes carry conserved syntenic anchors with small (~1–5 kb) variable cassettes between them. Catalogue every occupant across the corpus, then assign each occupant family a target defense system by testing whether its presence in phages tracks a specific defense system in those phages' hosts.
Humans miss it
It requires holding thousands of phage genomes × hundreds of host defense repertoires × positional context at once. The published instances each took lab-years for a single phage family.
Falsifier
Clone the ORF into a host carrying the predicted target system; challenge with a phage that system restricts. No ≥10-fold EOP rescue and it's dead.

Prior-art risk: HIGH. Hotspot detection is built (iLund4u); P2/P4 mining is published (PARIS). Defensible novelty is target assignment, not hotspot discovery. Verify in week one.

A2

The small-protein blind spot

Build
Finds
Anti-defense proteins that current methods structurally cannot see. Requires re-calling ORFs from nucleotides with no length floor, recoding-aware, scored by conservation and position rather than length.
Machines miss it
Not an oversight — a convention. The 100-codon floor is baked into every annotation pipeline and inherited by every downstream tool. Systematic blind spots are enumerable.
Caveat
Highest false-positive mechanism in the set; short ORFs occur by chance constantly. Viable only in hard conjunction with positional and selection evidence.

Prior-art risk: LOW–MEDIUM. The one I'm most confident is unexploited — precisely because the gap exists for an unglamorous reason.

A3

Coupling, with the phylogeny actually handled

Build
Finds
The seed's coupling mechanism — but the value is almost entirely in the correction, not the correlation.
The problem
Genomes are related by descent. A correlation across 400 genomes drawn from three clonal expansions has an effective n near 3. Uncorrected, this produces thousands of beautiful, significant, meaningless associations.
Expectation
Correction will kill most of the signal. That is the system working. If it kills all of it, A3 dies and the risk register says so.
A4

Trigger–antagonist pairing

Build, speculative
Finds
PARIS is the template: a defense system whose trigger is a specific phage counter-defense protein (T7 Ocr). That is a second-order relationship — defense₂ senses anti-defense₁ — and it is a grammar, not a one-off.
Falsifier
Express the candidate antagonist in a host carrying the candidate sensor. A true trigger should induce the defense phenotype in the absence of phage. Clean and unambiguous.
Honesty
My most speculative Tier-A entry, labelled as such. Depends on A1/A3 landing first — and carries the highest scientific upside in the catalogue.
A5

Selection signal on hypotheticals

Build
Finds
"This gene is under conflict." Homology-independent evidence of arms-race participation — it answers is this contested? without needing to know what the gene does.
Confounder
Phage genomes are pervasively mosaic and recombination badly inflates dN/dS. Recombination-aware methods are mandatory; anything that vanishes under correction dies.

Tier B  Conditional or corrective: corpus-wide recoding sweep; nucleotide-modification-based restriction escape; DGR-driven host-range plasticity (which phages can evolve new range — a better question than which they currently infect); module-boundary engineerability maps; and the lab's own EOP residuals, which are the single place private data beats the public corpus outright.

Architecture

One line does most of the work

Seven layers, but the design reduces to a single rule about where the language models are allowed to stand.

6QueuePre-registered claims: calibrated probability, explicit falsifier, costed experiment
5RankDeclared utility function; expected information gain per dollar
4Adversarial verificationTool-bound critics: prior-art gate, confounder attack, experiment-design critique
3SynthesisObservation → mechanistic claim + falsifier. The only layer where an LLM writes prose
Confounder gauntletNo language model may see anything below this line.
2SensorsStatistical detectors emitting observations — effect size, null model, corrected q, provenance
1RepresentationRe-called ORFs · embeddings · 3Di structure proxies · synteny graph · phylogenies
0SubstrateVersioned, content-addressed corpus snapshots

Data flows up · provenance resolves down · every queue item traces to content-addressed substrate

Why the line is there. Once a language model sees a spurious correlation, it will write a compelling mechanism for it. Narrative capacity is the enemy of calibration. So nothing reaches the narrative layer until it has survived the statistics — phylogenetic correction, sampling-power, redundancy, annotation-artifact, shortcut-learning and leakage checks, all automated, all before an LLM is invoked.

Why exactly three LLM roles. Because there are exactly three genuine information asymmetries, and every agent beyond that adds a silent-failure surface. The Synthesiser sees the evidence but is blinded to the literature, so it can't write toward a known answer. The Prior-art auditor sees the literature but is blinded to the claim's confidence, so it can't rationalise a high scorer. The Experiment designer sees the assay catalogue but not the novelty score, so it can't be seduced by excitement. Blinding as an architectural primitive, borrowed from experimental design rather than from agent frameworks.

Honesty machinery

How it avoids lying to the lab

GNoME and A-Lab did not fail at generation. They failed at novelty verification and at knowing what they had. That is the failure mode to engineer against.

The central idea

Run the entire stack — sensors, gauntlet, synthesis, critique, ranking — on a corpus whose biological signal has been destroyed but whose structure is preserved. Count how many compelling, falsifiable hypotheses come out. That number is the pipeline's false-discovery floor.

If the null corpus yields 40 exciting hypotheses and the real corpus yields 60, the system has produced roughly twenty findings and a great deal of prose. It is cheap, it runs every release, and it belongs at the top of the dashboard. I found no AI-for-science system that does this — and I think its absence is why the materials-discovery results were able to fail so publicly. Nobody asked their pipeline what it produced from noise.

The retrospective benchmark, and its contamination

Freeze the corpus at 2021-01-01 and require the system to surface what came after: PARIS (2022), Tad1 (2022), Acb2 (2023), the 21 defense systems that were never enriched in defense islands. Score by recall@k and by the rank of each — a system that buries PARIS at rank 4,000 has not rediscovered it.

The caveat must not be buried: ESM-2, ProstT5 and every frontier LLM were trained on post-2021 data. The benchmark is contaminated by construction. So the strict version runs on non-PLM detectors only — positional, phylogenetic, selection — computed from the frozen corpus alone. PLM-assisted results are reported separately and labelled contaminated, and the two are never mixed into one headline number. I would rather report a clean 0.3 than a contaminated 0.8.

The recommendation most likely to be resisted

If the lab only ever tests the top of the queue, the calibration curve is unidentifiable. You will learn your top bin's hit rate and nothing about whether your 0.3 predictions are really 0.3 — which is exactly what you need to trust the ranking. So: allocate 20–25% of experimental capacity to a stratified random sample across confidence bins, including bins you expect to fail. That is a real cost, and it is the price of knowing whether the machine works. A lab spending all its bench time on the top of the queue is running an unfalsifiable system, however good the statistics look.

Two more mechanisms close the loop. Every hypothesis is hash-committed before its experiment, and outcomes join on that hash — so post-hoc reinterpretation is structurally impossible and negative results cannot be quietly dropped. And each detector's realised hit rate is tracked against its predicted band; drift past tolerance triggers automatic quarantine — a code path, not a review meeting.

What a hypothesis costs to kill

You said you didn't know which assays are routine, so the menu is specified rather than assumed. Costs and durations are engineering estimates from published protocols, not cited figures — labelled rather than dressed up.

The dependency to flag plainly. Heterologous expression is the falsifier for A1, A2, A3 and A4 — the entire Tier-A programme. If the lab cannot clone and express genes in a tractable host, Tier A is not falsifiable in-house. That is not a reason to abandon it; it is a reason to partner, or to re-scope toward Tier B, which leans on EOP and sequencing you already do. Worth knowing before Phase 3, not after.

Roadmap

Five phases, each with a way to stop

Phase 1 · 0–3 moSubstrate and re-annotation

Corpus snapshots; recoding-aware, no-floor ORF re-calling; synteny and cassette atlas. Publishable as a community resource even if everything downstream fails.

Gate — measurable gain in coding density and recovered ORFs vs. GenBank
Phase 2 · 3–6 moSensors, gauntlet, retrospective benchmark

Certify each detector on structure-matched nulls, then run the 2021-frozen rediscovery test on the clean, non-PLM subset. This is the go/no-go.

Gate — clean recall meaningfully above chance, or stop entirely
Phase 3 · 6–9 moSynthesis, critique, queue

Three LLM roles, three-tier prior-art gate, pre-registration schema.

Gate — null-corpus yield is a small fraction of real-corpus yield
Phase 4 · 9–15 moBench loop and calibration

First pre-registered experiments, including the stratified calibration sample.

Gate — top-bin hit rate within defensible distance of 44.7%
Phase 5Expansion

Tier B mechanisms; the lab's EOP residuals; target assignment extended across host groups.

The risk I rate highest is not technical. It is that the system emits 200 well-formatted, well-cited, falsifiable hypotheses and the lab has bandwidth for six. A queue longer than throughput is a queue nobody reads. So the system should be tuned to emit roughly two to three times actual experimental capacity and no more — deliberately discarding real findings to protect the credibility of the ones it surfaces. That is an uncomfortable design choice and I believe it is the correct one.

If forced to pick one

Build the re-annotation first

A recoding-aware, no-length-floor re-annotation of the phage corpus, scored against λ's 55 known-missing ORFs as a positive control. It is cheap. It is verifiable against ground truth that already exists. It is useful to the community whether or not the rest gets built. And it produces exactly the substrate the anti-defense mechanisms need — because 80% of anti-defense proteins fall below the floor that every current pipeline imposes.

If that single artifact is all this project ever ships, it will still have been worth building.
